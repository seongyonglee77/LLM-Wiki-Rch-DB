The chapter shows that Akiko's storytelling developed from short, response-based tellings into longer, more complex, socially consequential narratives, while emphasizing that this development remained gradual, uneven, and interactionally demanding.

# Deep Summary

## Research Problem and Purpose

To document developmental changes in an L2 English speaker's interactional competence through the micro-practice of storytelling over five months of naturally occurring conversations-for-practicing.

## Theory & Literature Review

The chapter frames L2 learning outside classrooms as difficult to document but especially revealing when studied through conversation analysis, because storytelling requires coordinated use of turn-taking, grammar, recipient design, affiliation, and social action.

- Claim: Studying L2 learning outside the classroom is methodologically challenging because uncontrolled interactional experience may shape development.
  - Interpretation: The paper situates itself in SLA in the wild, where the researcher cannot isolate all learning influences.
  - Evidence: "Demonstrating how learning a second (L2) or additional language takes place outside of the classroom or in other words 'in the wild' (Clark et al. 2011; Wagner this volume) is especially challenging." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This establishes why the analysis focuses on observable changes in interactional practices rather than causal proof of learning mechanisms.

- Claim: The chapter adopts a conversation-analytic view of learning as embedded in recurrent social practices.
  - Interpretation: Learning is approached through participants' talk-in-interaction rather than through decontextualized linguistic outcomes.
  - Evidence: "In this perspective, learning is viewed as being embedded in the social and recurrent practices in which the non-expert L2 speaker participates." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This justifies tracking Akiko's changing storytelling practices as evidence of interactional development.

- Claim: Storytelling is positioned as a natural site for L2 learning because it joins language use with interpersonal relationship work.
  - Interpretation: The literature review treats storytelling as both linguistic practice and relationship-building activity.
  - Evidence: "Storytelling is a natural locus for L2 learning particularly in naturally occurring interactions as it allows non-expert L2 speakers to not only learn language but also to engage in interpersonal relationship work (Hellermann 2008)." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This connects the paper's microanalysis to broader SLA concerns about social affiliation and opportunities for participation.

## Findings

Across three interactions, Akiko moves from minimal, response-based storytelling to more sustained, multi-unit, humorous, and direct-reported-speech-rich tellings, while still struggling to project and maintain a complex story trajectory.

- Claim: In the first interaction, Akiko mostly remains a listener and produces only a very short story.
  - Interpretation: The baseline shows limited storytelling initiative and limited expansion beyond John's prompting.
  - Evidence: "Throughout interaction 1 Akiko mostly played the listener role, mainly producing one-unit turns, and rarely engaged in storytelling contrary to her co-participant." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This gives the longitudinal analysis a starting point against which later interactional gains can be compared.

- Claim: By the second interaction, Akiko produces more telling, longer turns, and more complex grammar than before.
  - Interpretation: The paper identifies development in both interactional organization and linguistic resources.
  - Evidence: "In this interaction, Akiko produces more telling than in the previous one." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This shows that the change is not only more talk, but a more sophisticated management of storytelling turns.

- Claim: By the third interaction, Akiko sustains an intricate extended telling, but does so in a fragmented and interrupted way.
  - Interpretation: The findings present development as uneven: Akiko gains resources but still has difficulty projecting the whole narrative trajectory.
  - Evidence: "The story emerges very gradually in a fragmented manner, as it is disrupted by three intervening sequences, and its structure is revealed upon analysis." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This nuance prevents a simplistic progress narrative and supports the chapter's claim that interactional competence develops gradually.

## Discussion

The discussion and conclusion interpret Akiko's development as evidence that storytelling offers a rich environment for practicing L2 interactional competence, while stressing that such development is slow, socially situated, and dependent on opportunities for recurrent affiliation with an expert speaker.

- Claim: Akiko's social relationship with John appears to support greater participation and more complex storytelling.
  - Interpretation: The analysis ties interactional development to evolving affiliation and shared history between participants.
  - Evidence: "Their  evolving friendship has direct consequences for the development of Akiko's L2 interactional competence inasmuch as her social affiliation with John provided opportunities  for  practicing  L2  (e.g.  telling  more  stories  and  producing  longer  and complex turns) thus opening new possibilities for language learning." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This strengthens the chapter's argument that L2 interactional competence develops through social relationships, not isolated practice alone.

- Claim: The author concludes that development was observable but difficult, incomplete, and not smooth after five months.
  - Interpretation: The conclusion explicitly resists treating the observed changes as full mastery of complex storytelling.
  - Evidence: "However, what also transpires from the data is that progressing along the interactional competence continuum is an arduous process." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This is important for using the chapter responsibly in literature review: it supports developmental evidence while preserving the limitations.

- Claim: Storytelling gives novice L2 speakers broad scope to develop interactional competence through complex social practices and grammar.
  - Interpretation: The conclusion generalizes from Akiko's case to storytelling as a fertile environment for L2 development.
  - Evidence: "Therefore, in providing a context in which extended turns and various social practices can be accomplished (e.g. joint remembering, doing humor, building up a climax, impersonating people's voices through direct reported speech) conversational storytelling represents a fertile  environment enabling non-expert L2 speakers to use a range of interactional  resources  together  with  a  more  complex  grammar." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This makes the chapter useful for arguing that personal narrative can be a pedagogically and analytically rich site for L2 learning.

## Unique Contributions

The chapter tracks one learner's storytelling over three recorded interactions across five months and links changes in turn-taking, TCU expansion, humor, direct reported speech, and social affiliation to observable development in L2 interactional competence.
