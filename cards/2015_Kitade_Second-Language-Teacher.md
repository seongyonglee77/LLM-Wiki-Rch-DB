# Quick Card

## Bibliographic Metadata

- Authors: Kitade, Keiko
- Year: 2015
- Design: qualitative case study

## One-sentence Summary

A 16-year narrative case study of two Japanese-language teachers shows that CALL agency develops as teachers negotiate technological contradictions, student needs, and community expectations through accumulated reflection and practice.

## Keywords

activity theory; teacher agency; computer-assisted language learning; trajectory equifinality approach; second language teacher development

# Structured Summary

## Purpose

To examine the challenges faced by two long-term CALL practitioners, how they responded to those challenges, and how those responses relate to the development of teacher agency.

## Findings

Both teachers developed awareness of technology's social nature by negotiating mismatched values with students. Toshi revised his online course after recognizing that students' low technology familiarity, rather than laziness, contributed to difficulty and dropout. Anne's accumulated CALL projects led her to distinguish students' technology preferences from broader social assumptions.

## Discussion & Conclusion

Teacher agency emerged as a mediating responsibility across communities with different technology values and expectations. The teachers' agency developed cumulatively through long-term interaction with teaching contexts rather than from one isolated event. The article concludes that ICT-mediated teacher agency involves actively interpreting and adapting technology to local environments.

## Discussion

- Teacher agency emerged as a mediating responsibility across communities with different technology values and expectations.
  - Evidence: "As they and their students negotiated values and expectations related to ICT, Toshi and Anne recognized their mediating mission: to respond to tensions between communities caused by the emergence of new technology." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)

- The teachers' agency developed cumulatively through long-term interaction with teaching contexts rather than from one isolated event.
  - Evidence: "It is also crucial to note that these teachers' responsibility as CALL practitioners has emerged not from a single experience of CALL practice but rather as the accumulated outcome of active interactions with teaching contexts over 16 years." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)

- The article concludes that ICT-mediated teacher agency involves actively interpreting and adapting technology to local environments.
  - Evidence: "The teachers in the present study have not simply been obeying social constraints and affordances: they actively understand the meaning of new technologies each in their own way and adapt their practices to their immediate environments; and in the process, they develop an articulated picture of their role as a CALL teacher in society." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)

## Limitations

This is a two-participant qualitative case study based on retrospective narrative interviews and context-specific CALL trajectories. The authors explicitly note that the participants' responses and implications are unique to their contexts and non-generalizable.

## Relevance to My Study

The paper is useful for research on language-teacher agency, CALL teacher development, activity theory, sociocultural mediation, technology adoption, and the negotiation of student needs in digitally mediated language education.
