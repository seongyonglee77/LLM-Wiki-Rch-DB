- Year: 2024

## One-sentence Summary

A six-week chatbot-assisted flipped approach improved elementary EFL learners’ interactive speaking and perceptions of learner–learner and learner–instructor interaction compared with worksheet practice.

## Keywords

flipped learning; chatbot; interactive speaking; EFL; mixed methods

# Structured Summary

## Purpose

To test whether chatbot conversation in the out-of-class phase can extend interaction opportunities in flipped learning.

## Method

Eighty-seven elementary EFL learners were assigned to chatbot or worksheet practice. Pre/post interactive speaking tests, an interaction questionnaire, and interviews were analyzed in a sequential explanatory mixed-methods design.

## Findings

The chatbot group outperformed the control group on interactive speaking and reported stronger interaction perceptions. Interviews explained this through repeated, low-pressure practice and continuity between out-of-class and in-class work.

## Discussion & Conclusion

Chatbots can bridge flipped-learning phases when tasks are aligned with classroom instruction; they expand practice but do not remove the need for teacher orchestration.
