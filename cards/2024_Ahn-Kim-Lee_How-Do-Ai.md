# Quick Card

## Bibliographic Metadata

- Authors: So-Yeon Ahn; Seonghoon Kim; Eun-Young Lee; Hyunjong Lee; Eun-Young Song; Junehwa Song; Jongseo Won
- Year: 2024
- Design: Conversation analysis of 200 recorded telephone interactions between human users and AI-based voice assistants in Korean customer service.

## One-sentence Summary

The study shows that AI voice assistants and Korean human users co-construct interactional roles through standardized AI prompts, honorifics, keyword-based responses, silence, and varying degrees of human-like conversation.

## Keywords

artificial intelligence; voice assistant system; adjacency pair; discourse analysis; positionality; human-machine interaction

# Structured Summary

## Purpose

To examine how AI-based service agents construct first pair-parts and how human users form second pair-parts in customer service encounters.

## Findings

The AI always opened the interaction with the same first pair-part, regardless of user needs. The AI's opening combined a feminized voice and a recommended speech style for users. Keyword responses were the dominant user second pair-part. The authors found four main user response types, and the pattern held across successful and failed cases.

## Discussion & Conclusion

Human users' keyword responses may reflect beliefs about how AI understands language. Honorifics and address terms contributed to positioning, distance, and formality in human-AI interaction. Embedding polite and hierarchical forms in AI speech may reinforce social structures and shape user perceptions. The study calls for systematic design attention to AI service agents' conversational engagement.

## Findings

The findings show that AI agents produced standardized first pair-parts while human users responded in four main ways: no verbal input, keywords, natural/casual talk, and direct demands modeled on the AI's suggested style. The most frequent pattern was keyword response, and the Korean honorific system shaped interactional positioning.

- Claim: The AI always opened the interaction with the same first pair-part, regardless of user needs.
  - Interpretation: The standardized opening positioned the AI as the sequence initiator and constrained what counted as a preferred user response.
  - Evidence: "In these encounters, FPP is always performed by the AI-based service agents, articulating the identical remark regardless of the counterpart and their needs." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This is the central empirical basis for describing the AI as shaping the interaction before the user speaks.

- Claim: The AI's opening combined a feminized voice and a recommended speech style for users.
  - Interpretation: The service agent did more than greet; it modeled how customers should formulate the next turn.
  - Evidence: "This standardized FPP is conveyed through a distinctly female voice, re fl ecting a broader industry trend of designing AI products, such as chatbots and robots, with feminine characteristics." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This supports the claim that interface design choices participate in social positioning and not just task routing.

- Claim: Keyword responses were the dominant user second pair-part.
  - Interpretation: More than half of the 200 cases involved users reducing their response to topics or keywords rather than full sentences.
  - Evidence: "Among the total 200 instances, there were 106 cases (54 %) in which the human users answered with topics or keywords without any other syntactical elements." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This is the strongest quantitative finding in the source and anchors the interpretation that users often treat AI interaction as search-like.

- Claim: The authors found four main user response types, and the pattern held across successful and failed cases.
  - Interpretation: The response typology is not presented as an artifact of success/failure labeling by the provider.
  - Evidence: "Notably, the general trend across the four types of reactions observed was consistent regardless of whether the cases were deemed successful or failures." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This makes the typology useful for summarizing interactional behavior across the dataset as a whole.

## Discussion

The discussion argues that Korean honorifics and AI prompt design create shifting social hierarchies between service agents and users. It also stresses that conversational interface design should account for culturally embedded interactional pragmatics and that some findings require further contextual and cross-linguistic investigation.

- Claim: Human users' keyword responses may reflect beliefs about how AI understands language.
  - Interpretation: The paper interprets keyword use as potentially shaped by users' expectations that AI should be addressed like a search engine or computer system.
  - Evidence: "Among the four types of SPP observed, the human users most frequently responded to the AI ' s FPP with keywords, potentially signaling their underlying beliefs in how they should communicate with AI-based service agents, providing keywords as if searching online to most e ff ectively and e ffi ciently retrieve and access the information they wanted and meet their needs." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This links observed turn design to broader user models of AI, making the finding relevant to interface design and user education.

- Claim: Honorifics and address terms contributed to positioning, distance, and formality in human-AI interaction.
  - Interpretation: The Korean linguistic system is treated as part of the interactional infrastructure through which users and AI are socially positioned.
  - Evidence: "Here, the linguistic indexicality, particularly the use of honori fi cs and address terms, contributed to the positioning and distance betweentheAI-based voice assistants and the human users as well as the formality of the conversation." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This is the paper's main culturally specific contribution and cautions against designing AI dialogue as culturally neutral.

- Claim: Embedding polite and hierarchical forms in AI speech may reinforce social structures and shape user perceptions.
  - Interpretation: The authors treat language design as consequential for how people interpret and engage with AI agents.
  - Evidence: "By embedding hierarchical and polite forms in AI communication, service providers may inadvertently reinforce social structures, thus a ff ecting users ' perceptions of and interactions with technology." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This turns a linguistic finding into a design implication for customer-service AI.

- Claim: The study calls for systematic design attention to AI service agents' conversational engagement.
  - Interpretation: The conclusion moves from descriptive CA findings to applied recommendations for conversational-interface design.
  - Evidence: "The study suggests that service providers need to pay attention to how their AI-based service agents interact with and engage in conversations with their customers in a more systematic manner." (page unavailable; source-text-verified)
  - Why it matters: This supports the summary's relevance to both AI research and applied linguistics.

## Unique Contributions

The paper contributes a conversation-analytic account of how AI-led openings and user second turns position human and machine interlocutors in Korean customer service calls.

## Limitations

The source notes that telephone data excluded nonverbal cues, silence is hard to interpret without fuller situational context, and future work is needed across languages without Korean-style honorific systems.

## Relevance to My Study

Relevant to human-AI interaction, applied linguistics, Korean honorifics, customer-service voice assistants, conversation analysis, and culturally situated conversational-interface design.

## Future Work

Future studies should examine human-AI interaction in languages without honorific systems, investigate contextual reasons for no-response cases, and study how natural AI service agents should sound to human consumers.
