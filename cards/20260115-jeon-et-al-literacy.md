## One-sentence Summary

The framework argues that AI literacy must move beyond tool use toward critical, situated, and inclusive meaning-making in which learners infuse human experience and validate outputs in real contexts.

## Keywords

AI literacy; New Literacy Studies; inclusion; critical thinking; meaning-making

# Structured Summary

## Purpose

To develop an educational framework for understanding AI’s effects on literacy, equity, human agency, and meaning-making.

## Method

Conceptual synthesis of literacy studies, critical thinking, media/cultural education, and scholarship on generative AI.

## Findings

AI’s fluent and anthropomorphic output can encourage uncritical acceptance, homogenization, and inequity. The proposed response emphasizes audience, production, critical evaluation, infusion of meaning, and action/community validation.

## Discussion & Conclusion

Students are not competing with AI as text generators; they should use it as raw material while contributing history, judgment, creativity, empathy, and situated knowledge.
