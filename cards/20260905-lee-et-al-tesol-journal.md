## One-sentence Summary

The review argues that Gemini Live’s live voice, vision, and screen sharing can support authentic, scaffolded communicative tasks, but multimodality alone does not make an activity task-based language teaching.

## Keywords

Gemini Live; multimodal GenAI; communicative language teaching; TBLT; task design

# Structured Summary

## Purpose

To examine Gemini Live’s pedagogical affordances and constraints for communicative language teaching and task-based interaction.

## Method

Media review of Gemini Live’s multimodal functions, illustrated through object-based interaction, restaurant ordering, and local-place presentation tasks.

## Findings

Camera, voice, gesture, and screen-sharing afford integrated reading, writing, listening, and speaking, contextual follow-up questions, information gaps, rehearsal, feedback, and reflection. Carefully designed prompts and lesson sequencing are required.

## Discussion & Conclusion

The tool is best treated as a resource within a teacher-designed task cycle; collaborative screen writing may support planning or reflection rather than constitute a task itself.
