One-sentence Summary

A systematic review of 32 empirical studies finds rapid growth in speech-recognition chatbot research, with perception and speaking/listening outcomes dominating and conversational-partner use most common.

Keywords

speech-recognition chatbot; automatic speech recognition; language learning; systematic review; LLM

Structured Summary

Purpose

To map publication, participant, methodological, technological, and pedagogical patterns in speech-recognition chatbot research for language learning.

Method

Thirty-two empirical studies were reviewed across publication information, research focus, setting, participants, duration, target language, devices, methods, and chatbot roles.

Findings

Research increased sharply from 2020, focused mainly on college ESL/EFL learners, and commonly used mixed methods and mobile devices. Studies emphasized learner perceptions and speaking/listening development; the chatbot was usually a conversational partner.

Discussion & Conclusion

The review establishes a baseline for evaluating newer LLM-plus-speech systems and calls for broader designs, clearer chatbot descriptions, and stronger attention to learner, technology, and context factors.
