One-sentence Summary

Across 11 experiments, chatbots matched human interaction for some linguistic outcomes but were weaker in sustained interactivity, elaboration, and socially supportive communication.

Keywords

chatbot; human-chatbot comparison; language learning; systematic review; interlocutor

Structured Summary

Purpose

To synthesize experimental comparisons between human and chatbot interlocutors in language education.

Method

Eleven experimental studies and 64 statistical findings were narratively synthesized across 11 outcome variables because study conditions were too heterogeneous for meta-analysis.

Findings

Chatbots produced comparable utterance sophistication, vocabulary/grammar levels, and some speaking/listening gains. Humans were generally stronger for sustained interaction, informative elaboration, and maintaining positive long-term engagement.

Discussion & Conclusion

The authors propose treating chatbots as novel interlocutors rather than imperfect human simulations, with human teachers focusing on relational, interpretive, and pedagogical work.
