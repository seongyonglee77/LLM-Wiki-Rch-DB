One-sentence Summary

This conceptual article extends Darvin and Norton's model of investment through a critical sociomaterial account of human-GenAI interaction, arguing that agentive language learning requires learners and teachers to examine how identity, resources, platform design, algorithms, and ideology distribute and constrain agency.

Keywords

generative AI; language learning; learner identity; investment; distributed agency; critical digital literacies; sociomateriality

Structured Summary

Purpose

The article asks how learners can invest in agentive GenAI practices that support meaningful learning and their own intentions without delegating the production of meaning and texts entirely to technology.

Findings

Agency in GenAI-mediated learning is relational rather than unilateral learner control. GenAI platform designs contain internal logics and biases that shape identity and interaction. Training-data inequalities reflect uneven global knowledge production and affect human-AI interaction.

Discussion & Conclusion

Agentive co-construction requires learners to retain responsibility for meaning-making and reflexive learning. Classroom GenAI use should be aligned with learning objectives and function as a scaffold rather than a substitute.
